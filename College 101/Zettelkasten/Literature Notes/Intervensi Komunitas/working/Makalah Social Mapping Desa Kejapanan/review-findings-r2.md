1. **High | Demonstrable inconsistency: the urgency comparison does not apply the same age-limit logic to both organisations.**  
   **Location:** §6.3, M1 U and M4 U, lines 291 and 318; §6.4, urgency paragraph, line 356.  
   M1 receives U4 because members eventually exceed the age limit, whereas M4 receives U3 because young people supposedly can participate later. However, the paper itself gives Karang Taruna an upper membership age of 30. Its members can also age out during inactivity. The statement that postponing M2–M5 “tidak menghapus kesempatan pihak yang terdampak” therefore exceeds the evidence. Furthermore, §7.4 acknowledges missing membership data, so “setiap tahun sebagian anggota” is not established through the reported age distribution.  
   **Why it matters:** This is the central answer to the lecturer’s urgency challenge, yet it does not establish why M1 is comparatively more urgent.  
   **Correction direction:** Distinguish the inherently limited period of childhood from demonstrated imminent losses among actual members. Apply the same time horizon and evidence standard to M4, and qualify claims about annual losses until membership ages are known.

2. **High | Evidentiary weakness: M1’s highest seriousness score converts possible intervention benefits into established consequences of inactivity.**  
   **Location:** §6.3, scoring anchors and M1 S, lines 279–292; §6.4, lines 354–360.  
   S5 requires effects extending to at least two other problems. M1 qualifies through its supposed effects on Karang Taruna and cross-dusun participation. However, §6.4 describes these connections as potential, long-term benefits. The supplied field findings establish that the three problems coexist, but do not establish that Forum Anak inactivity produces Karang Taruna stagnation or village-wide fragmentation. McMillan and Chavis provide a theoretical rationale for interaction and belonging, not evidence that this particular forum will reduce territorial fragmentation.  
   **Why it matters:** The scoring rewards the proposed solution’s promise as though it were demonstrated problem severity. Repeating those same assumptions as an additional justification in §6.4 does not independently corroborate the ranking.  
   **Correction direction:** Separate observed harm, plausible causal connections, and prospective benefits. Explain that counting connections to other MAP problems is the group’s operational choice, and test whether M1 remains first when uncertain connections receive less weight.

3. **High | Demonstrable reasoning defect: M3’s consequences are artificially restricted to meeting participants.**  
   **Location:** §6.1, M3 K; §6.2, M3 justification; §6.3, M3 U/S, lines 309–310; §6.4, M3 decision.  
   The paper reasons that deliberation still produces decisions, so postponement causes no meaningful loss, and that its effects are confined to participants. Yet §4.5 describes village decisions and the difficulty of bringing marginalised groups’ voices into those decisions. People absent from a meeting can be affected by its decisions. Producing decisions also does not establish that exclusion is harmless.  
   **Why it matters:** This systematically discounts a competing participation problem while treating children’s lost participation as consequential. It weakens the fairness of the whole comparison.  
   **Correction direction:** Distinguish whose participation is restricted from whose interests the decisions affect. State what consequences remain unknown instead of inferring low harm from continued meetings or the absence of conflict.

4. **High | Demonstrable inconsistency: the Growth anchors cannot reliably distinguish persistence from deterioration.**  
   **Location:** §6.3, G anchors, lines 282–284; M1 G, M2 G, M4 G and M5 G, lines 293, 302, 320 and 329.  
   G4 includes becoming permanent, G3 includes structurally persisting, and G2 includes remaining stable. M1 receives G4 for “menetap sebagai formalitas,” while M2 receives G3 for “cenderung menetap.” These describe substantially overlapping trajectories. M5’s explanation that opportunities can be developed later also resembles the U3 anchor, despite receiving U2.  
   **Why it matters:** Explicit anchors create an appearance of comparability without resolving the underlying distinctions. The available one-period assessment does not establish contrasting growth trajectories merely by using different descriptions of persistence.  
   **Correction direction:** Define an observable distinction between persistence and worsening, use a common time horizon, and identify which trajectory judgments are uncertain. Reconcile M5’s delayed-benefit explanation with the urgency anchor.

5. **High | Evidentiary overreach: forum inactivity is expanded into an unmet participation right generally.**  
   **Location:** §6.3, M1 U/S, lines 291–292; §6.4, line 356.  
   “Hak partisipasi anak belum terpenuhi” and members reaching adulthood “tanpa pernah mengalami partisipasi yang bermakna” exceed the demonstrated finding that this forum is inactive. The assessment did not interview children and did not establish their participation experiences in schools, families, religious groups, or other settings. “Satu-satunya wadah anak” likewise lacks a reported inventory establishing exclusivity.  
   **Why it matters:** The child-rights justification is defensible at the level of an unavailable village participation channel, but becomes vulnerable when it implies comprehensive deprivation. Desa Ramah Anak status does not independently establish that broader conclusion.  
   **Correction direction:** Bound the harm to participation **through Forum Anak**. Retain the rights rationale without claiming that children have never experienced meaningful participation elsewhere or that no alternative channel exists.

6. **High | Demonstrable inferential leap: legal membership is treated as an organisational transition and capacity pipeline.**  
   **Location:** §6.4, leverage paragraphs, lines 358–360.  
   Permensos 9/2025 establishes automatic Karang Taruna membership from ages 16–30. It does not establish that “seluruh anggota beralih ke Karang Taruna setelah berusia 18 tahun” in the sense of active organisational involvement. The paper then moves from that assumed transition to more capable personnel for the café and hydroponics, without evidence of recruitment, willingness, relevant skills, or management arrangements. The facilitator discussion also omits Permen PPPA 3/2025 Article 12(3)–(4), which permits exceptions to the age ceiling and former-Forum-Anak-management requirement.  
   **Why it matters:** The strongest claimed leverage pathway relies on automatic membership being mistaken for active recruitment and competence.  
   **Correction direction:** Distinguish legal membership, voluntary involvement, and demonstrated capacity. Present the alumni pathway as conditional, include the relevant facilitator exceptions, and avoid making future village labour supply a principal justification for children’s participation.

7. **High | Evidentiary weakness: the feasibility argument does not address the governance changes the paper itself identifies as necessary.**  
   **Location:** §6.1, M1 L5; §6.4, lines 362–364; §7.4, lines 377–381.  
   M1 is considered maximally feasible because an SK, adult companions, and representatives already exist. However, §7.4 identifies unclear funding, missing membership data and evaluation, inadequate planned support, and children’s exclusion from decisions. Designing activities with children does not by itself establish that the group can change these arrangements. The sentence that stagnant Karang Taruna means “tidak ada pihak yang menahan kemunduran ini” also overlooks the already identified government and school companions.  
   **Why it matters:** The paper discounts M2 and M4 because they depend on institutional coordination, while understating similar dependencies for M1.  
   **Correction direction:** Specify the achievable intervention scope and the adult commitments it requires. Separate feasible initial facilitation from sustained governance reform, and explain how existing companions can support it.

8. **Medium | Demonstrable analytical weakness: the fishbone partly explains inactivity with inactivity and overstates its causal hierarchy.**  
   **Location:** Gambar 4; §7.4, paragraphs 1–3, lines 375–379.  
   “Kegiatan tidak rutin” closely restates the outcome “rendahnya keaktifan.” The text then calls two categories roots because they explain “sebagian besar penyebab lain,” although it explicitly recognises that time pressures arise independently and does not demonstrate that those roots produce spatial fragmentation. “Karang Taruna stagnan” is placed under “desa terpecah per wilayah,” but no evidence is given that territorial fragmentation explains its stagnation.  
   **Why it matters:** Accepting all 20 items as field findings does not establish their causal order. The lecturer’s concern about imposed categories can recur when category names do not adequately explain their contents.  
   **Correction direction:** Distinguish symptoms, intermediate mechanisms, and underlying conditions. Explain the basis for prioritising the two proposed roots, qualify “akibat langsung,” and reconsider or explicitly justify the placement of Karang Taruna.

9. **Medium | Demonstrable scope inconsistency: the demographic target includes people excluded by the paper’s own definition of children.**  
   **Location:** §3.2, “Usia 8-18,” line 104; §5.2 M1; §6.1–6.2 M1; §6.3 and §6.4 age definitions.  
   The paper labels 4,142 residents aged 8–18 as the direct Forum Anak target, but later defines children as under 18 and officeholders as 12–17. The earlier assessment’s footnote confirms only the arithmetic, 2,063 plus 2,079. It does not resolve the age-boundary mismatch or identify actual forum membership.  
   **Why it matters:** The number supports the problem’s population reach while mixing a demographic bracket, eligible children, and forum participants.  
   **Correction direction:** Retain 4,142 only as the available demographic bracket, explicitly distinguish it from eligible or enrolled participants, and identify the underlying table’s provenance and year. Do not silently recalculate an unavailable under-18 count.

12. **Medium | Demonstrable reporting inconsistency: the stated data period excludes evidence actually used.**  
    **Location:** §1.3, line 47; §2.1, line 63; §3.1–3.2.  
    §1.3 sets the secondary-data period at 2024–August 2026, but the methodology and demographic analysis explicitly use BPS data for 2022, published in 2023. Separately, the 2026 village proposal reports population “pada akhir Bulan Desember” without specifying the year. Its publication date therefore cannot establish the observation year.

13. **Low | Methodological reporting gap: “partisipatif” is asserted without explaining participation in the analysis.**  
    **Location:** §2.1, opening paragraph; §6.1–6.3 scoring procedures.  
    The paper identifies four adult informants and explains data collection, but does not say who generated the problem list, assigned scores, resolved differences, or checked interpretations with community members. Interviews establish community input, but do not explain participation in prioritisation.  
    **Why it matters:** The lecturer cannot tell whether the scores represent group judgments, informant judgments, or a jointly agreed assessment.  
    **Correction direction:** Report the actual analytical procedure and who participated. If students conducted the scoring, state that directly and distinguish it from community validation.

14. **Low | Subjective editorial preference: some signposting repeats structure instead of advancing the argument.**  
    **Location:** §1.2, final paragraph, line 39; §6.4, opening paragraph, line 354.  
    The five-step chapter roadmap repeats the immediately preceding objectives table. In §6.4, announcing three considerations and then explaining that each answers a different question delays the substantive justification already signalled by the following paragraphs. “Kerugian yang tidak dapat diulang” is also imprecise: the intended meaning concerns an irrecoverable opportunity, not a loss that cannot recur.
