1. **High impact, evidential overstatement: the fishbone synthesis presents proposed causal relationships as established findings.**  
   **Location:** §7.4, paragraphs 1–3, especially “Kesenjangan ... inilah yang membuat kegiatan tidak rutin” and “Gejala pada kategori individu ... merupakan akibat dari cara forum dijalankan.”  
   **Evidence:** Pambudi’s slide 9 describes fishbone as identifying *potential* causes. The group’s fishbone supplies an explanatory model, while §2.3 acknowledges limited observation and insufficient confirmation from children. Neither establishes that governance caused all individual factors, particularly time occupied by school. The underlying fishbone table says “sebagian besar” individual factors are consequences, whereas the synthesis generalizes across the category.  
   **Why it matters:** The central intervention rationale becomes more certain than the assessment supports.  
   **Correction direction:** Preserve the proposed governance explanation, but distinguish observed conditions from hypothesized mechanisms. Limit the consequence claim to the factors actually explained and identify what requires confirmation with children.

2. **High impact, conceptual inconsistency: “interest” changes meaning between actors.**  
   **Location:** §4.3, operational definition, table rows 8–11, and the first explanatory note below the table.  
   **Evidence:** Interest is defined as “tingkat kepentingan ... untuk terlibat dalam dan mendukung pembangunan.” UMKM receive high interest because they stand to benefit, while Forum Anak and Karang Taruna receive low interest because activities are inactive. The note explicitly substitutes “keterlibatan kelembagaan yang teramati” for interest in those two cases. Meanwhile, §7.4 explains inactivity through constrained opportunities and governance.  
   **Why it matters:** The horizontal axis compares different constructs. Inactivity can coexist with substantial interest, including at the institutional level. The distinction between institutions and individuals does not resolve this.  
   **Correction direction:** Preserve the agreed placements as provisional group judgments, but explicitly identify activity as an imperfect proxy and apply consistent criteria across actors. Do not present inactivity alone as establishing low interest.

3. **High impact, justification defect: several power ratings do not follow the document’s own definition of power.**  
   **Location:** §4.3, table rows 4, 8, and 9; §4.5, paragraph 2.  
   **Evidence:** Power includes resources, networks, mobilization, and formal authority. Yet low power for UMKM and women’s representatives is justified principally through absent formal authority. BPD is rated as an institution, but its justification relies on individual members lacking village-wide dominance. The supplied UU 6/2014, Article 55, gives BPD collective functions concerning village regulations, aspirations, and oversight. Territorial representation does not itself establish territorially limited institutional authority.  
   **Why it matters:** These explanations risk understating informal influence and confusing an institution’s power with that of individual members.  
   **Correction direction:** Keep the selected ratings provisional, distinguish BPD’s collective authority from individual influence, and explain what evidence supports limited practical influence across the stated dimensions. Where evidence is absent, say the influence remains insufficiently assessed.

4. **Medium impact, unresolved scope contradiction: the village-wide assessment does not align with the adjacent population statement.**  
   **Location:** §1.1, final paragraph; §1.2, goals 1–3; adjacent §1.3, “Populasi.”  
   **Evidence:** Suji’s sections describe mapping village demography, actors, resources, and problems before selecting a priority. Section 1.3 identifies the population solely as “Anggota Forum Anak Desa Kejapanan.”  
   **Why it matters:** Readers cannot determine whether Forum Anak was the original assessment population or the focus selected through APKL and USG. This changes how the assessment sequence should be understood.  
   **Correction direction:** Reconcile the group-level scope statement by distinguishing the initial village assessment from the subsequently selected intervention focus. This is already recorded in the working notes but remains unresolved in the assembled draft.

5. **Medium impact, source application exceeds the available detail: the Hart comparison lacks evidence about the participation process.**  
   **Location:** §7.4, paragraph 3, “Kondisi Forum Anak di Kejapanan mendekati pola tersebut.”  
   **Evidence:** Hart describes tokenism through apparently granting children a voice without meaningful choice, and illustrates manipulation through consultation without feedback or transparent use of contributions. The field notes establish complaints that aspirations were not answered, but the working file explicitly acknowledges that the consultation process was not documented in detail.  
   **Why it matters:** Even “mendekati” links the forum to specific participation categories without showing the corresponding process. Inactivity and unanswered aspirations alone do not establish both patterns.  
   **Correction direction:** Use Hart as an analytical framework for questions requiring confirmation, or restrict the comparison to the specific feature supported by the field findings.

6. **Medium impact, inaccurate regulatory paraphrase: the description of eligible village-level companions omits an express alternative.**  
   **Location:** §7.4, paragraph 2, “menetapkan perangkat pemerintah desa sebagai pendamping di tingkat desa.”  
   **Evidence:** Permen PPPA 3/2025, Article 10(a), specifies “perangkat pemerintah desa/kelurahan **atau seseorang yang telah memenuhi syarat**.” Article 11 supplies those requirements.  
   **Why it matters:** The shortened account suggests a narrower appointment rule than the source establishes, particularly beside the discussion of SDN Kejapanan V’s supporting role.  
   **Correction direction:** Include the alternative eligibility route and distinguish the regulation’s formal *pendamping* role from the school’s reported involvement.

7. **Medium impact, citation attribution defects: the news source has a named author, and one citation appears to support more than the article contains.**  
   **Location:** Daftar Pustaka, Jatimsatunews entry; corresponding citations in §§1.1 and 7.4; final sentence of §7.4.  
   **Evidence:** The supplied article identifies **Anis Hidayatie** as its author. Its page headline is “Kejapanan-Gempol, Desa ke-20 Dinilai Lomba Kampung Pancasila: Harmoni Warga dan 4 Gereja”; the bibliography instead uses an alternate heading appearing within the article. The final sentence of §7.4 also places one news citation after a list containing SDN Kejapanan V’s accompaniment and representatives in every dusun, neither of which appears in the article. Those details come from field records.  
   **Why it matters:** Authorship is misattributed, and readers cannot reliably trace the mixed-source asset list.  
   **Correction direction:** Use the named author consistently, reconcile the reference title with the cited page, and separate field-derived assets from the article-supported claims.

8. **Medium impact, ambiguous terminology at the handoff: “akses antardusun sulit” leaves the type of barrier unspecified.**  
   **Location:** §7.4, paragraph 4, against adjacent §3.1, “Aksesibilitas dan konektivitas.”  
   **Evidence:** Section 3.1 describes relatively easy transportation and mobility. The interview evidence supporting §4.5 concerns territorial agendas, coordination, and difficulty assembling participants. Section 7.4’s unqualified “akses” can instead imply physical transportation barriers.  
   **Why it matters:** The distinction affects whether an intervention should address transport, scheduling, outreach, or institutional coordination. The two descriptions are not necessarily contradictory, but the current wording does not explain their compatibility.  
   **Correction direction:** Specify the documented barrier to participation. Retain a physical-access claim only if supporting field evidence identifies it.

9. **Low impact, editorial preference: repeated closure weakens the economy of the fishbone synthesis.**  
   **Location:** §7.4, endings of paragraphs 1 and 3, followed by the root-cause formulation in paragraph 4.  
   **Evidence:** “Kondisi tersebut bersumber dari kategori lain,” “Gejala ... merupakan akibat dari cara forum dijalankan,” and the final root-cause statement repeatedly conclude that individual difficulties arise from organizational conditions.  
   **Why it matters:** The paragraphs repeatedly announce the same interpretation instead of progressively developing its mechanisms. This is a localized formulaic pattern, not evidence of model authorship.  
   **Correction direction:** Retain one explicit synthesis and use the preceding paragraph endings to clarify distinct mechanisms or evidential limits.
