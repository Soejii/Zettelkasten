## Evidence gaps
- No measured sugar content or cup volume for jumbo cups sold in Surabaya. The 60 g figure assumes the same 600 ml cup as the Kemenkes test.
- No Indonesian study found linking temperature to sweet-drink intake. He dkk. (2025) is US data.
- No study found applying HBM to sweet drinks in Indonesian young adults, so the HBM slide is labelled a hypothesis.
- Primary sources behind Buwana (2023) (Akhriani 2016, Zoellner 2012, Riebl 2015, Wahidah & Rahayu 2022) were not inspected. They are cited as secondary.

## Argument plan
1. Anecdote opens the question, labelled as non-evidence.
2. SKI 2023 shows daily sweet-drink intake is common, but Jawa Timur sits below the national rate. The distinctive part is portion size and the heat trigger, which SKI does not measure.
3. Sugar load: two 600 ml cups pass the 50 g limit (writer's arithmetic from S4).
4. Heat: Surabaya temperatures (S5) and US evidence on heat and added sugar (S6), with the setting caveat.
5. TPB explained (S7), then mapped onto the case with literature for each component (S11 to S13).
6. Strength of TPB: variance explained (S15) and most consistent predictors (S14).
7. Limits: Ogden on eating behavior (S8, S9), including the tension with Buwana's finding that intention predicts SSB behavior.
8. HBM as a hypothesis for low concern (S10, S16).
9. Conclusion bounded by the evidence.
